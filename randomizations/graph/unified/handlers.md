# entity

Not ready for release

Changes how entities are acquired (see lib/logic/acquisition.lua). So far build slots (which item places which entity) and autoplace slots (which entity map generation puts where), matched one to one: a random matching of admissible bases, committed through promotion's try_rewires a cycle or chain of slots at a time.

Pairings between slot kinds follow `acquisition.pairing` (lib/logic/acquisition.lua), which also gives each connection's abilities (`entity.connection_abilities`, see "Connection abilities" in docs/glossary.md), and whether it takes killing a carrier (`needs_kill`), in which case the head connects to the slot's carrier base instead (see Spawn slots).

Autoplace slots: autoplaced entities found in exactly one room (except resources, cliffs, plants, spawners, worms, fish, and ones mining which unlocks a technology) trade slots, taking the whole autoplace spec of the entity they replace and its place in the planet's map generation settings. An autoplaced entity nothing promised needs can stop being autoplaced (its head is detached). Built entities can instead take an autoplace slot and be salvaged: found in the wild for the neutral force, which can't be used there, and mined for a new item that places them (only entities with one placer, not needed in bulk, that fit where the slot's entity was). Logic's entity-own node keeps this honest: only what's ours can be operated (see docs/glossary.md).

Spawn slots (only with the biters setting, propertyrandomizer-unified-entity-biters): what a unit spawner spawns. Units trade spawn slots (a traveler keeps the slot's spawn points), a built entity can instead be carried by biters (a carrier: the slot's unit with the entity's look, dropping a new item that places it as loot; its head connects to the slot's carrier base, a spoofed base needing the slot's source and damage the unit's resistances let through, since the loot takes killing the carrier), and a unit can also be placed by an item (a friendly biter). Units' placing slots are spoofed edges whose heads start detached (starts_detached, see promotion.new), so logic never counts on friendly biters: the pool graph and promotion don't connect them, and first pass gets its graphs with them cut (gutils.detach_starting_heads). Moving a slot never drops what else mining the entity gave, like a plant's fruit (common.swap_mining_items), since logic keeps those edges. A slot whose unit stopped spawning at some evolution (transient) keeps spawning its new unit at every evolution, at no less than a tenth of its highest weight; late slots are gated by enemy-evolution, so only travelers nothing needs end up there.

Trigger slots: what using a capsule, firing ammo, or capturing a spawner makes for us (edges tagged ours into entity-own), like the combat robot capsules, the capture robot rocket, and spawners turning into the captive biter spawner. Only entities with health count, since ones without (like the atomic bomb's smoke) are only for looks. What they make trades among them or stops being made (if nothing promised needs it), and a built entity can instead be made by one (only minable ones with one placer, a capsule or ammo costing within 4x of its item, a captured spawner only for ones needed a few at a time that fit where the spawner was): it's ours where it's made, and mined for a salvage item. Capsules are never spoof placers though (their use can be bound to left click, which places buildings), so a capsule makes a building only by being thrown. The item's create-entity effects are rewritten on renamed copies of what it sends, so a shared projectile doesn't change for anything else (common.retarget_created_entities); a building is made once at a free spot for whoever used the item (common.create_as_building). The item keeps its look with a badge of what it makes, and a spawner's description says what capturing it makes.

Spoil slots (only with the biters setting, since eggs hatch enemies) and dying slots: what an item spoils into (like a biter egg hatching a big biter) and what an entity leaves behind when it dies (like a stomper's shell or a demolisher's corpse). What they make trades among slots of the same kind or stops being made (if nothing promised needs it). A built entity can take a spoil slot as a carrier, hatching as a hostile copy of the egg's unit that drops its item (worth killing, like spawner carriers), or a dying slot as a wreck (a neutral simple entity with its look, mined for a salvage item; the dying enemy has to be worth killing for it). Spoil bases belong to the item's identity (identity_base), like build bases. Spawners, and what asteroids break into (anything that spawns in space, and what that dies into, over and over), stay put.

Capsule effects: capsules thrown for an effect (a throw action with a range, whose use makes nothing with health, like grenades and poison and slowdown capsules) get spoofed trigger bases (maker bases, from an edge to a spoofed entity-own node), which a built entity or what a trigger slot makes can take: the capsule then makes it where it's thrown, instead of its effect. The displaced effect moves to the start of the chain of slots ending at the capsule: a Vestige, which then places an invisible explosion running the effect as its created_effect (it can't be a ghost, so no blueprints), or a combat robot capsule nothing else changed, which throws the effect instead. Otherwise a Vestige without an effect gets it, or it's lost if there's none. Effect capsules nothing else changed also trade effects among each other (a rotation among about a quarter of them). Capsule effects aren't in logic (only combat robots are damage sources), so moving them needs nothing from promotion. Logic has an item-capsule node for every capsule, so capsules that spawn nothing (like the poison capsule, whose cloud is a create-smoke effect) can have bases too.

Balance (step 6): each built entity has a demand tier (acquisition.demand_tier: bulk for the classes in acquisition.bulk_entity_types or an item stacking to 100 or more, few for one stacking to 5 or less, some otherwise). Bulk entities are never salvaged, but can be carried by biters, since with entity randomization on, logic has a group-supply mechanic per bulk class (lib/logic/entity-supply.lua) that keeps some member of the class suppliable automatically wherever vanilla had one. Carriers keep their unit's health, only carry items worth killing them for, and drop more of cheap and bulk items (acquisition.loot_amount). Salvage is never worth more than 25 times what mining the slot's entity gave (acquisition.worth_salvaging); a pairing over that is refused, not scaled down. Items without a known cost are never carried or salvaged, since neither can be balanced without it. Each seed logs FARMREPORT lines: what's only gotten by hand, from where, and for bulk classes with such members, which members items still place.

Items that place nothing in vanilla (plain items, modules, ammo, science packs, and other item types that aren't used by clicking) can also take a build slot, as spoof placers: they keep their look, with a badge of the entity's icon. Every item still places at most one entity, so the chain of items taking each other's entities that ends at a spoof placer starts at an item that now places nothing (a Vestige, darkened and pointing to the spoof placer). See docs/glossary.md.

Mining back: mining a claimed entity gives back the item placing it now (reflect), so logic's edge from mining an entity to its vanilla item is claimed too (a mine-back edge) and follows the build matching. When a group of build slots commits, its mine-back changes go in the same try_rewires: an item a slot takes is mined back from that slot's entity (from nothing if the entity isn't mined back, like a unit), and a Vestige is mined back from nothing. So the original game keeps what mining buildings back gave (like an item on a space platform that places something built there), and promotion protects it. Items that placed nothing in vanilla (spoof placers) get no mine-back edge, and an entity several items place has its edge dropped, both of which only make the model pessimistic. Reflect checks that the model's mine-back edges match what mining gives.

With first pass and item randomization: which entity an item places belongs to the item's identity, so build edges are tagged identity_base and mine-back edges identity_head (first pass moves them with the trav), and first pass doesn't split build slots or mine-back edges (they're on its blacklist). It reflects before item randomization, which copies item names and icons into recipes.

Entity positions (only with constants.entity_first_pass, off for now): first pass matches this handler's slots as its own positions, and the handler's own matching (above) doesn't run. A position is an orand fed by one of this handler's heads (entity_positions), except mine-back and carrier edges. Its vanilla identity is the entity its head leads to, or nothing for the build positions of items placing nothing in vanilla (spoof placers), the maker bases of effect capsules, and nowhere positions: NUM_NOWHERE_POSITIONS spoofed edges whose heads start detached, so an identity first pass puts there stops being acquired (detached), and units' placing positions work the same way. Positions are all orands, so first pass matches any identity to any position its rules allow (first_pass_rules): the pairing has to be one logic can model (acquisition.pairing and validate on the base it connects to), built entities keep their items' costs within constants.entity_cost_tolerance, and every kind change passes the checks above (fits, worth killing or salvaging, minable, one placer, demand tiers) and the identity's own draw of its *_CHANCE. Nothing can go anywhere (an item placing nothing, a spot nothing is found in), a built entity never goes nowhere, and something found in the wild, spawned or made by a trigger may for DETACH_CHANCE of them. A connection gains or loses what the pairing does, like a built entity found in the wild or looted not being automatable, and a carried entity's starts at the slot's carrier base (monotone matching's params.connection, which also checks needs where the connection starts). custom_prereq_search then gives each identity's head the base of the position first pass put it at (the carrier base for carriers; none at nowhere positions), which is all reflect reads, and promotion needs nothing from it, since first pass's split graph already has the positions connected. A spawn, trigger, spoil or dying slot first pass leaves with nothing keeps making its vanilla entity in the game (reflect only rewrites slots something moved into), which only makes the model pessimistic. It's off because matching both at once crowds out item randomization: an entity's build position hangs off its item's identity, which moves in the same proposal, so first pass's proposals keep breaking (every round lost the agricultural tower on Gleba) and the needs its fixes add pin item identities (3 of 8 resource slots got a new identity instead of 8 of 8, and loads took about four times as long). A fix to try: match items first, then entities with the item assignment fixed.

# spoiling

Not ready for release

Behind its own setting, propertyrandomizer-unified-spoiling (visible so the user can try it, off by default), even while dev-unified is on, since the checkout is the user's playable mod (user, 2026-09-29). It only takes effect with dev-unified or the unified preview, and tests/configs.txt pins it on for unified-all.

This handler matches spoil slots itself, so it keeps the orands of its edges out of first pass (first_pass.blacklist), like the entity handler. Otherwise first pass makes each claimed spoil edge's orand a slot and trades what spoil edges lead to among themselves, which reflect never applies, and its model loses what the game keeps (with plant harvesting automatic, Gleba's bacteria spoiling into ore, which left first pass moving no items).

Which items spoil into items and what they spoil into, in both directions, like mining-fluid-required: spoofs give items that could start spoiling a base (an edge into a spoof sink), and items that could become spoil results extra result slots. The new slots copy vanilla's result counts (like spoilage's 8 spoilers) onto NEW_RESULT_COPIES sets of random items. Every result slot, vanilla or new, is filled with FILL_CHANCE, and every vanilla spoiler keeps spoiling with it, so there are about twice as many spoilers as in vanilla, and spoilage is one of several common results rather than always the common one. Cycles are fine.

An item's spoil result is an OR prerequisite of the item it makes, so an empty slot is a detached head, not a base fed by true (which would make the item free). So the search is custom (custom_prereq_search): each choice is committed through promotion's try_rewires, and a vanilla slot a promise needs (like bacteria spoiling into Gleba's only ore) keeps its vanilla spoiler, replacing the old name blacklist. The spoofed edges start detached (starts_detached), so the pool graph, first pass and promotion never count on them until the search fills them.

Spoil bases belong to the item's identity (identity_base, so first pass moves them with the item), and result slots are positions. Reflect writes spoil results as position names before item randomization, which renames them to the items first pass put there. Spoil times belong to the item: a vanilla spoiler keeps its own, and a new one takes the spoil time of a vanilla spoiler that lasts a trip to another room (see dutils.survives_trip), so no item stops being deliverable. Spoiling into an entity (like a biter egg hatching) is the entity handler's; an item can have both.

Items that never spoil or become results: hidden ones and ones that only live in the cursor (only-in-cursor or spawnable flags). Armor with an equipment grid never spoils, and results must stack.

Spoil results need a sink, a way machines can use them up on the way to research (lib/item-sinks.lua; not the same as being useful elsewhere): a path of steps has to end at a science pack, which labs use up, or at chemical fuel some burner can burn. Placing an item, launching it for products, other fuel categories and items used directly (ammo, capsules, modules, ...) don't end a path, since their use can't be automated or is hard to. Steps are recipes some machine can craft, hand-written recycling like scrap recycling included, and spoiling; the recycler's generated recycling isn't a step. The whole game counts, wherever and whenever a step can be done. Each new result is a group of NEW_RESULT_CHOICES random items, drawn first from items with a sink in the game as it is (most final products have none), and the search fills the slots of the first one with a sink, judged on the game reflect will build (recipes stay with positions, while ending a path and spoiling move with the identity first pass put there), or else of the first one that can be a result at all. Once every handler's changes are in (after_changes), any spoil result without a sink burns as chemical fuel, even one the recycler takes: like the vanilla spoil result that burns as chemical fuel and that the most items spoil into (spoilage in Space Age), or, if it's already a fuel of other categories, keeping its fuel value. That covers vanilla results whose position holds another identity, and sinks that recipe or entity randomization took away after the search.
