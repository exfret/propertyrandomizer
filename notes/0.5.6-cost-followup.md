## Cost comparison: not uniformly equivalent

Two separate comparisons were made:

1. Price the **same unrandomized prototypes** with the old flow solver and fixed resource table from **`3a26519^`**, then with current pricing. This isolates the cost-model change without mixing in changed recipes.
2. Run current **recipe-only randomization**, with unified disabled, on base and Space Age seeds **749, 750, 751**. Reprice before and immediately after recipes/recycling, before numerical randomization. Compare each pricing model to itself. Also price both sides with the same old resource weights and current solver, excluding recycling on both sides.

These are internal modeled production costs, not measured playtime, transport expenses, or a full optimization of an actual factory. This is not a distributional benchmark of recipes generated by the old release. In particular, current Space Age pricing has distinct local-only and import-enabled tiers; comparing different tiers gives misleading ratios.

### Model changes on identical recipes

- **Base:** current home/local prices have a median **1.115×** ratio across 185 materials priced by both models. 172/185 are between 0.8× and 1.25×. The six science packs priced by both models are **6–13% higher**.
- **Uranium is an exception:** uranium-235 rises from the old explicit 100-unit price to roughly **1,195**. Its fixed-price shortcut was removed along with the hand-authored resource tables. This propagates into nuclear products; it is not a small global rescaling.
- **Space Age:** the median current home-price ratio is **2.585×** across 248 jointly priced materials. This is partly the deliberate move from globally cheapest recipes to prices in the material's home planet. It is not numerically equivalent to the previous global model. For example, unchanged automation science prices 2.81× higher, while electromagnetic science prices 0.257× as high.
- **Coverage also changed:** the current automated-only raw table does not price several biological materials. Agricultural science, for example, is priced by the old model but has no current aggregate/home price in these runs. The ordinary recipe randomizer's cost-availability gate skips recipes with unpriced ingredients (`randomizations/graph/recipe.lua:222–235`). This comparison therefore cannot certify balance for every Space Age recipe.

Space Age cannot be described as uniformly preserving costs. Its import-enabled comparisons can change the cheapest interplanetary supply routes; their ratios differ substantially from the local-price targets used during recipe selection.

### Why seed 750 reports a 41× cryogenic price

`context-costs.lua:321–336` prefers the local-only price at a material's home whenever it exists, even if the full/import-enabled tier is much cheaper. In this seed, after recipe randomization:

- Cryogenic science's home/local price is **1,006.37**, compared with **24.35** before: **41.33×**.
- Its import-enabled price is **29.46**, compared with **10.74** before: **2.74×**.
- The search prices its chosen ingredient batch at **56.03**, against its staged target of **55.84**. Its small reported drift therefore does not establish that the finished game's cost is unchanged under either full-game comparison.

The selected ingredients are 55 solid fuel, 5 plastic bars, and 6 cold fluoroketone. The current solver splits recipe cost across the pack and its hot-fluoroketone coproduct (`flow-cost.lua`); the game's original outputs were checked in `/Applications/factorio.app/Contents/data/space-age/prototypes/recipe.lua:853`.

The discrepancy survives repricing with a tenfold flow-iteration budget. It also reproduces with **no diagnostic pricing before randomization**. It comes from the distinction between pricing tiers and staged recipe targets, not merely a diagnostic side effect or evidence of a mandatory 41× gameplay expense. The earlier commentary estimate of +21% compared an import-enabled result with a local-only baseline and is superseded by the like-for-like 2.74× figure above.

The policy at `context-costs.lua:321–336`, plus staged imports priced from the unrandomized set at `recipe.lua:323`, deserves a separate balancing review if preserving costs is a release requirement. No balancing policy was changed in this audit.

## Missing-pack investigation and correction

Space Age's installed biolab prototype lists **12 science packs**, including promethium (`/Applications/factorio.app/Contents/data/space-age/prototypes/entity/entities.lua:1772`). “11 initially reachable” described a limitation of the old graph, not the actual number of packs or proof of complete progression.

- **Old reachability omits promethium.** `lib/old-logic/build-graph.lua:3633–3651` builds chunk sources only from directly spawning asteroid chunks at space locations. The promethium chunk source has no prerequisites/alternatives, because this graph omits the larger-asteroid destruction route. The installed asteroid generator produces chunks through `create-asteroid-chunk` death effects (`space-age/prototypes/entity/asteroid.lua:257`). The restrictive source scan also exists at the audit baseline `bcb14f7`, so this particular blind spot predates the reviewed commits. A report of 11/11 only establishes preservation of those eleven modeled packs.
- **The current pricing filter excludes biological production.** `lib/logic/concrete.lua:27` explicitly sets `AUTOMATABLE_PLANT_HARVESTING = false`, introduced by `9fd85d69` as a workaround for unified matching. `context-costs.lua:73–79` and `graph-cost.lua` use automatability to filter raw inputs for ordinary recipe pricing as well. The unrestricted graph has prices, but the automated recipe-cost model loses the required inputs. This affects agricultural science and promethium's biological ingredients even with unified disabled.
- **Agricultural science has an additional self-sustaining-production gap.** Enabling harvesting in a temporary snapshot restores promethium's import-enabled baseline price to **43.7061**, but agricultural science remains unpriced. Every prerequisite of the pentapod-egg recipe is then classified as automatable except the input egg itself; that blocks both the egg recipe and agricultural science. The installed recipe consumes one egg and returns two (`space-age/prototypes/recipe.lua:382–409`). The sort does not recognize the automation enabled by starting that multiplying loop with a manually obtained egg.

No gameplay/model switch was changed during this investigation. Both diagnostic snapshots deliberately stop after collecting pre-randomization data; their intentional diagnostic errors are not ordinary mod startup failures. Logs and scripts are in `/tmp/propertyrandomizer-science-gaps/`.

The cost test was corrected to fail missing baselines, with a regression assertion for that behavior. Re-evaluating the six existing run logs with the corrected checker gives **three base passes and three Space Age coverage failures**, with seed 750 additionally failing the individual and mean bounds. The reported 10-pack averages are incomplete; the full 12-pack average remains unknown until pricing is repaired. This supersedes the earlier “5 of 6 pass” claim.

### Why agricultural science is priced at 0.76

The baseline flow calculation is 0.37 for bioflux + 0.10 for an egg + 4 × 0.07 for recipe time + 0.01 for complexity = **0.76** per pack. Ingredient amounts, time and output were verified in `/Applications/factorio.app/Contents/data/space-age/prototypes/recipe.lua:800`. Bioflux is 1.48 per four-output recipe, hence 0.37 each. The egg breeding recipe's computed batch cost is 1.74565, but the cheaper raw egg source wins.

There is a separate, concrete undercount in the raw egg source. `lib/logic/concrete.lua` builds loot edges from `entity-kill` to the item without an output `amount` (marked `TODO: cost amounts`). The graph-price solver treats unmeasured AND outputs as free, so these paths charge only the item's 0.10 base cost. The earlier detailed trace shows the two spawner-kill edges win over the much more expensive corpse-mining path. The installed enemy prototypes specify nonzero egg loot (`space-age/prototypes/entity/enemies.lua:6153` and `:6334`), but those amounts are not transferred into the pricing graph. This was diagnosed, not changed, during the user's question about the low number. Restoring prices does not correct this separate loot-cost model gap.
